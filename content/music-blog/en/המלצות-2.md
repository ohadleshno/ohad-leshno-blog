I got severe food poisoning from a trip to the cave this week, so I finally have time to write the recommendations I collected. (You know the goddesses who have to push the fact that they travel in everything they do? Well, because I don't). We have a lot of worthwhile recommendations, so let's get started:

<figure class="my-8"><img src="https://static.wixstatic.com/media/cd66a9_ed048c0a012541ddae325b10304f4610~mv2.jpg" alt="" class="w-full h-auto rounded-2xl shadow-lg border border-slate-200 dark:border-slate-800 object-cover max-h-[550px]" /></figure>

Well, you can put the cards on the table straight, this week we experienced some huge mental upheavals, this feeling that in a second you are going to be shot at by missiles from Iran is not a very pleasant feeling to say the least. In moments like these, I always remind myself that in the end, everything will work out. Despite the gloomy predictions, things tend to work out for the better in the end, as Alon Ader said:

<blockquote dir="auto">
"Another year has passed, no disaster has happened
But the newspapers insist that here it comes
The end of all ends, the end of all men
The boy stayed, the rain returned
And by and large, this world works despite everything."
</blockquote>

It still amazes me to think that the next day we all got up to work as if nothing had happened. I love this Israeli resilience, the ability to always move on, what a wonderful people we have.

And a little escapism of a Jew who works overtime from the other side of the ocean, the album Only God Was Above Us by Vampire Weekend was released, which will probably easily be one of the successful albums of 2024. It's good that there are bands today who are not afraid to release an 8-minute song (hope) and not fit into a 3-minute format in order for it to enter the playlists. Throughout the album, the hand of the band's vocalist Ezra Kinning, who is a musicologist at the highest levels who also has a very successful podcast about music, can be felt. A few weeks ago they asked in 20 questions "What are the first names of the super group CSNY"? (Crosby, Stills, Nash and Young). For me it was a shame that they asked such a basic question, it's like not knowing the first names of the members of the Beatles. But of course it took me a second and I came out of my excessive douchebag and realized that there are people who were not exposed to the Flok phenomenon that changed music forever and that to say that it is the same as the Beatles is a bit exaggerated. So for those who don't know, here is the song deja vu from the masterful album deja vu that always makes the heart feel good.

And in the same breath it should be noted that recently a new country song TEXAS HOLD'EM for Beyonce was released. I actually want to emphasize his instrumental version which is simply great, it puts the banjo in the center and gives it the respect it deserves. Beyond the fact that the song is simply great and the production is excellent, what is more interesting is that Beyoncé is the first black woman in history to reach number one on Billboard's Hot Country Songs chart. Which is a kind of historical correction to the cultural appropriation that the genre passed from the black community to the white working class in the USA. Kudos to her, not obvious at all especially in the current climate in the US.

And speaking of the black community recently, while I was going to buy flowers in the Carmel market one of the sellers put on the song All night long by Lionel Richie. One of the songs that started a genre of introducing Caribbean influences into pop in a quite successful way in my opinion. Somehow during the small talk I always have with any seller in the world, she told me that my dad was crazy about this song at my age. I told her when my father was my age this song hadn't come out yet. She couldn't believe it, we did the math and she was so excited that I was right that she wanted to bring me the flowers for free. (Of course I paid in the end). I also threw her a cute fact at the end that the words:

"Tam bo li de say de moi ya"

that are thrown there are complete gibberish and that he did not have the strength to hire a translator to bring him something really in a real African language.
