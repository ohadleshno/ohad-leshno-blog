I got severe food poisoning from a cave hike this week, so I finally have time to write down the recommendations I collected. (Know those people who have to mention they hike in everything they do? Yeah, me neither). We have plenty of great recommendations, so let's get started:

<figure class="my-8"><img src="https://static.wixstatic.com/media/cd66a9_ed048c0a012541ddae325b10304f4610~mv2.jpg" alt="" class="w-full h-auto rounded-2xl shadow-lg border border-slate-200 dark:border-slate-800 object-cover max-h-[550px]" /></figure>

Well, we can lay our cards on the table right away: this week we experienced immense emotional turbulence. That feeling that missiles might be fired at you from Iran any second is not very pleasant, to say the least. In moments like these, I always remind myself that in the end, everything will be alright. Despite grim forecasts, things tend to turn out for the best eventually, as Alon Eder said:

<blockquote dir="auto">
"Another year has passed, no disaster occurred
yet in the newspapers they insist it is coming:
the end of all ends, the demise of all people.
The child remains, the rain returned
and overall, this world works despite it all."
</blockquote>

It amazes me to think that the next morning we all woke up for work as if nothing happened. I love this Israeli resilience, the ability to always keep moving forward: what a wonderful nation we have.

And a bit of escapism: Vampire Weekend released the album "Only God Was Above Us", which will easily land as one of the best albums of 2024. It is great to see bands today not afraid to release an 8-minute song ("Hope") rather than fitting into a 3-minute template just for playlists. Throughout the album, you feel the hand of lead singer Ezra Koenig, a top-tier musicologist who also hosts a great music podcast (<a href="https://music.apple.com/us/curator/time-crisis/993269786" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 underline hover:text-indigo-500 font-medium break-all">Time Crisis</a>).

A few weeks ago, a trivia quiz asked "What are the first names of the supergroup CSNY?" (Crosby, Stills, Nash, and Young). For me it felt like a scandal to ask such a basic question, like not knowing the Beatles' names. But of course after a second I stepped back from my over-the-top music snobbery and realized that some people haven't discovered the folk phenomenon that changed music forever. For those unfamiliar, here is the track "Déjà Vu" from the masterpiece album Déjà Vu, which always warms the heart.

In the same breath, we should mention Beyoncé's country track "TEXAS HOLD 'EM". I want to highlight the instrumental version, which is wonderful: putting the banjo front and center with proper respect. Beyond the track being great and the production top-notch, Beyoncé became the first Black woman in history to hit #1 on Billboard's Hot Country Songs chart. This marks a historical milestone in reclaiming country music's roots.

Speaking of music history, while buying flowers at Carmel Market recently, one vendor was playing "All Night Long" by Lionel Richie, one of the songs that pioneered blending Caribbean influences into pop. During our casual conversation, she told me my dad used to be crazy about this song at my age. I told her that when my dad was my age this song hadn't come out yet! She didn't believe me, we checked, and she was so impressed that I was right she wanted to give me the flowers for free (I paid in the end). I also mentioned that the phrase:

"Tam bo li de say de moi ya"

is complete gibberish because Lionel Richie didn't feel like hiring a translator for a real African language!

And finally, last week marked 30 years since the single "Supersonic" shattered charts and crowned the Gallagher brothers from Manchester as one of the biggest bands in British history. To mark the occasion, they re-released a live performance from 30 years ago at the Limelight in Belfast. Pure 90s rock after the grunge era. Fantastic performance, hope you enjoy it!
