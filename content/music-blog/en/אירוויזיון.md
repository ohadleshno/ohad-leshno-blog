This week the Eurovision Song Contest started, an event that used to not be an event only for people with a sexual orientation to the same chromosomal composition or for generic mutants with zero musical sense. This concept of a music competition is essentially strange, in the first place how can music be measured on such a broad level, and also why is the competition by country? Especially since today everyone just sings in English songs that could just as well have been generated by AI. But, despite all my excessive fussing, I still feel that there is some kind of magic in it, magic like in musicals that take you back to being a little kid who wants to know how the story will end and that the hero will win. A childish and innocent feeling with a hint of optimism and absolute escapism. And today I would actually like to persevere in some of those points that were in Israeli culture from the competition, from those times when we had some self-respect and we would sing in Hebrew and not in English with a bad accent. But, before that I have to share a story.

There was a place near the Wix offices in the harbor where we would occasionally have lunch. The place is some kind of home-cooked food place that everyone would give a different name according to their seniority in the company. There used to be hummus with low hygiene, so there were a couple of friends who called it "diarrhea hummus" and someone else called it Falafel Gabai, give me a guess why. Near the above mentioned place was a jewelry store of Yizhar Cohen. At that time there was someone on my team who had a partner in the company and together they would look and act like the old men from the Muppets. On the way to one meal, they explained to me the concept they called being "implicitly annoying", basically it means doing annoying things but not in a direct way that would hurt a person. A type of trolling that only works indirectly, for example, standing in the middle of the elevator instead of on the side when someone comes to enter. They were very pleased with these jokes. Almost regularly, on the way back to the office after everyone was exhausted from the restaurant's salty food, they would stand by Yizhar's store when he was inside and talk loudly to each other in order for Yizhar to hear: "I don't believe that miracles have been done in Yizhar Cohen's store." No doubt they were legends in their lifetime.

As you know, Yizhar won the Eurovision Song Contest with the song "Avnivi", but Yizhar appeared one more time at the Eurovision Song Contest with the song "Oleh Oleh" in 1985. In those days they probably liked to dress like the Power Ringers and disco was at its peak. This lovable Yemenite could have won one more time if it wasn't for Ahbela who screamed her life out when he sang Oleh Oleh. (minute 1:02)

Luckily her microphone flew off in the middle of the song so it got to the fifth place and not the 25th place. By the way, he is the only singer sent twice to Eurovision apart from Ilanit and is really very similar to Nisim Grama. (but he doesn't have falafel)

Another singer I really like and discovered thanks to the Eurovision is Avi Toledano who is one of the most charismatic singers here in Israel, he appeared in the Eurovision with the song Hora which was covered by none other than Nancy Brands. (Yes, the one with the plank jokes). The song won Doz Pua from Finland, which in hindsight, it turned out that the Finnish judging team really liked Toledano who sang in loud "whore" ("parent" in Swedish) voices. The song Hora is really another temporary classic and as with any classic, the microphone must be kicked in the middle of its performance by one of the dancers in the show in order not to give us the first place. (minute 2:02)

What I really love about the song is the infinite class that Toledano has, without pyrotechnics, without glittering screens, he simply manages to conquer the screen in a way that no artist today in Eurovision is approaching.

Many know that "Diva" was Israel's third Eurovision win, written by Zvika Pick and performed by Dana International. But, not many know that Zvika is also the artist who appeared in the most "pre-Eurovision" competitions. Pick participated no less than seven times in the competition, but did not win any of them.  One of the coolest and most special songs he released is the song Romance, in his performance of which he was very influenced by Sergeant Pepper and dressed in those royal military uniforms.

Another excellent band that appeared at Eurovision is none other than Beehive, which appeared in 1974 with the song "I gave my life". The regulations of the competition at that time allowed only six band members to go on stage, but Beehive had seven members, so of course I would not if Yoni Rechter had to become the champion of the band. The song itself reached the seventh place, while the first place was reached by Abba's song "Waterloo". It's crazy to think that a song like this and to think that the songs that are in the Eurovision today are still known and loved compared to all the songs from the last few years that disappear in a minute.

But the funniest thing in the world that happened was the sending of the ping pong band to the Eurovision in 2000, which really seemed like the State of Israel had given up on the Zionist enterprise already at that stage. It is not only unbelievable that they were so unaware and incapable of singing and doing any kind of performance, it is more impressive that the Broadcasting Authority even let them enter the competition. They are falsifying levels and at some point they start waving Syrian flags in a section that is not clear at all, beyond the fact that the song is perverted and lacks a high point. It's a very fine rendition of the tarla at a very low level the way I like it. There was a stage when the Broadcasting Authority realized what it had done and wanted to sue them for a refund and disowning them, but it's already too late after a few waving of the flags of an overly hostile country. By the way, the guy's fake tattoo is from table tennis.
