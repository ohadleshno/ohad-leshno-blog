When I want to listen to music, I act like a <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greedy_algorithm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 underline hover:text-indigo-500 font-medium break-all">greedy algorithm</a>. Whatever is most accessible to me that passes my threshold is what I will listen to. If I am at home and there is a record already on the turntable, I will put it on. If I have an album in Apple Music that was started and paused in the middle, I will continue listening to it. Usually I do not even think before I play music, I simply continue whatever was there before. There are periods when I find myself listening to the same albums over and over again, dozens of times, until I move on to the next thing. The beautiful side of this is that every album is linked in my mind to a specific period. For example, this album is linked to February 2020, to my trip to Budapest, exactly one month before the outbreak of the COVID pandemic. But as usual, let's do a brief review of the artist first.

Geva Alon was born and raised in Kibbutz Ma'abarot. He began playing guitar at age 12. In 1999, he began his journey as the frontman for the band "The Flying Baby" (<a href="https://www.barby.co.il/showcat.aspx?id2=4812" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 underline hover:text-indigo-500 font-medium break-all">who are playing at the Barby soon</a>). One of the things that helped Geva Alon break into public consciousness was his cover of David Bowie's "Modern Love". I feel that many times covers miss the mark by not bringing anything new, and if they do bring something, it would have been better if they hadn't. I cannot understand the love surrounding all the reality TV cover songs. Here Geva Alon brings the song in a genuinely new variation, as if it was written originally for him. Besides that, I love that he published it like a boomer with a picture of roses without even stripping the wmv extension from the name. He didn't do the bare minimum, and that is charming. It feels as if I accidentally stumbled into a video explaining how to configure network settings on a computer by some virgin kid managing a MapleStory forum on FXP.

Back to my personal story: although I love adventure, traveling, and world experiences, I always find myself questioning whether I am truly enjoying myself. I have a mistaken perception that when I am abroad I must enjoy myself, and if I am not enjoying myself, I try forcefully to enjoy, which causes me even more despair. When I was a counselor in Bnei Akiva, it was easy to identify the kids who wanted attention. They did everything to get a bit of attention, but the more annoying they behaved, the less attention they received from counselors. Their behavior was annoying and off-putting, and the harder they tried, the less they received what they wanted. To get the attention they wanted, they needed to break that cycle and realize that was not the right way. That was how I felt about my trip. I realized I had to be less achievement-oriented, that there is no right or wrong answer, the only question is what I want to do. But when you stand in front of that thought honestly, many fears surface. What if I don't like doing anything? I thought that if I don't like traveling, I am a boring and meaningless person condemned to live a boring life, because in everyone's eyes traveling is one of the most important things in life. The social achievement mindset took up a huge volume in my mind. My coping mechanism was to try harder, as I was raised that if I didn't succeed it was only because I didn't try enough. I went to another museum, another bar, but each time the goal did not achieve its purpose, similar to that kid in Bnei Akiva. You cannot force yourself to enjoy, I had to learn to let go. That evening we reserved a spot at a restaurant, got ready in our Airbnb, and the realization landed on me that I was hurting myself. I simply sat down and put this album on the TV, and it was a sweet comfort, and I told myself that I wanted to stay in the apartment. It was the best evening of the trip. Funny in retrospect how the world signaled to all of us a month later that we must stop in such a symbolic way with a pandemic.

I think it is no coincidence that this album gave me the ability to stop for a second and breathe. It opens with "The Great Enlightenment", establishing a thoughtful tone that permeates all the other songs on the album. Alon's guitar work is exceptional, weaving intricate patterns that are both calming and evocative. His voice, with its warm resonance, carries a sense of earnestness and intimacy, inviting the listener on a personal journey. It allows the listener to truly connect with themselves for a second and be with themselves in the moment. This reaches a peak in the album for me in the title track, "Grey Herons". Meticulously written and beautifully performed, with poetic words of hope and renewal. The sparse arrangement allows Alon's voice and guitar to fill the space with emotional resonance.

<blockquote dir="auto">
"In the morning light
i wish i was
in the morning light
as the years come tumbling down
i wonder if i'll hear the sound
of the grey herons flight
in the morning light"
</blockquote>

All the other songs on the album are wonderful. In particular, "I See the Love" showcases Alon's ability to blend folk and rock elements seamlessly. His use of reverb on the guitar creates an atmospheric sound, while the delicate use of slide guitar adds a touch of fun Americana.

The album closes with "Rock All Night", which is a tribute to classic rock with a folk touch. This song features a powerful rock sound with a full band, adding a gritty texture to the album. It is a song calling to celebrate life, packed with vibrant energy and raw electric guitar solos.

Before my flight to Vietnam, I went to his concert at Nocturno in Jerusalem, one of the best shows I have seen recently. He is a virtuoso in a way that has no equal in Israel in my opinion, a one-man show that feels like a full band. During the performance, he gave a speech where he shared that this was his first show since the war began. You could see his pain, his concern, his humanity; he was exposed and honest, bringing his whole self to the stage. And there I was, experiencing that familiar feeling again, like back on vacation, wondering what I was doing. I started asking myself: am I even enjoying this? Is it allowed to enjoy in the current situation in the country? But this time I came prepared. I calmed myself with the inner reminder that it is fine if I go to a concert and do not enjoy it. If it isn't good, everything is still fine, I can let go. At that moment something magical happened: I gave myself the ability to truly release, focus on the music, and forget that thought as if it never existed. It was a closing of the circle for me once again with Alon.
