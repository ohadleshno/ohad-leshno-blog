This time I decided that instead of an album I will pick a motif I noticed recently that appears in many songs I love. The motif we are washing over today is none other than rain. I won't write fancy text about rain: if that is what you are looking for, ask ChatGPT. Today I will present rain in its pure emotional form through music, so let's get soaked and explore rain's different facets.

The first track in the playlist is the song that inspired this idea, so we begin with Yoni Rechter's "Rain Falling" (VeGeshem Yored). I believe I am not the only one who has felt lonely, isolated, and stuck. Yoni knows how to create the exact atmosphere with piercing lyrics. This song is so sharp and wonderful that it immediately washes over you. It comes from the album "Intentions" which I wrote about previously.

<blockquote dir="auto">
"And the rain keeps falling
finding its way to the sea
and I have nowhere to go
no place in the whole world"
</blockquote>

To bring holiness to the world we must include at least one Grateful Dead track. We get soaked by "Looks Like Rain" in the Dick's Picks 19 performance, and we also get "Box of Rain" in the studio version fitting our context. Two contrasts of the same entity: one chaotic, heavy, vulnerable, and piercing, speaking of unrequited love, and the studio version, organized and restrained, speaking of rain as healing for the soul. Like rain itself, organized chaos is expressed everywhere with the Dead.

<blockquote dir="auto">
"A box of rain will ease the pain
And love will see you through"
</blockquote>

And speaking of the Grateful Dead, we must mention Robert Zimmerman (Bob Dylan) who brings another dimension of rain in one of the greatest protest songs of all time, "A Hard Rain's a-Gonna Fall", which became the soundtrack for the civil rights movement and the voice of a generation.

When I think of songs about rain I always ask whether I have ever seen the rain, referring to "Have You Ever Seen the Rain?", where rain in this context represents depression on a sunny day. The song was written during the breakup of CCR.

There is another song I want to mention before concluding, which might be the most surprising track on the playlist: "Rain Over Me". Its purpose is to remember that music can be silly and fun. It reminds me of high school, connected to times when I went to see Fast & Furious 5 at the Rav Chen cinema.

This playlist concludes with Keren Peles' "Mabul" (Downpour). Like my own life full of cynicism, surprises, and constant character breaks, I had to finish the playlist with humor and good taste. The song I chose for this ending is my personal victory song, and anyone who knows me well knows that I used to know all the lyrics by heart.

Because I am not the type of person who listens to only one genre, I included many great anecdotes from diverse musical worlds in this playlist. This playlist was assembled by searching through my saved music library. Every song here represents a story and a complete experience in my world. Rain is a dimension that allows creators to build an experience that feels subjective to us. With so many forms of rain, open your heart and discover a whole world.

Enjoy the music.
