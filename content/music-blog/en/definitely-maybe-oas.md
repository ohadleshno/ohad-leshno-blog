Today is my older brother Ari's 40th birthday, so after a long time without writing a blog post, I found the strength to write a dedication to the amazing person who raised me and gave me a warm place in the world. Ari is the person who taught me the most important things in life: he taught me what music means, rooted in me a love for video games, and turned me into the proud nerd I am today. He ignited my first love for music that I listened to as a kid, from Bob Dylan who became my guiding light to Oasis who were his favorite band. So today I will write about the album that will soon mark 30 years since its release, Definitely Maybe, which is one of his and my favorite albums.

Before we start talking about the album, a little background on the band: the band consists of two brothers, Liam and Noel Gallagher, who grew up in Manchester, British lads in every sense of the word. In one of their early gigs they smashed up the pub, because why not. I recommend doing a Google search on them: the insane number of articles written about them to this day is mind-blowing to say the least. They were rockstars in the fullest sense, sex, drugs, and rock 'n' roll, full of hubris claiming they were bigger than the Beatles. Unfortunately over the years the two became bitter enemies, the band split, and since then they have performed separately. This album was the breakthrough for the brothers who would one day conquer the world and cause one in six Britons to try to buy a ticket for their Knebworth concert, one of the biggest gigs in history. They performed in front of 500,000 people over two nights, during a time before the internet existed in its current form. And today Liam finds himself needing to make tea for himself because people don't buy records anymore.

The album "Definitely Maybe" landed at a critical moment for the British music scene, offering a refreshing alternative to the grunge movement that dominated at the time. For those unfamiliar, grunge music generally consists of dirty guitar tones, heavy riffs, and driving drums. Oasis, led by brothers Liam and Noel Gallagher, blended a unique mix of rock and pop, creating a fresh sound that spoke to an entire generation. This album arrived right after the end of Nirvana when the world was waiting for something new.

The album is characterized by bold guitar riffs, straightforward drum patterns, and Liam Gallagher's distinctive vocals. Songs like "Rock 'n' Roll Star" and "Cigarettes & Alcohol" demonstrate the band's blunt and unapologetic approach to rock music. Noel Gallagher's songwriting skills shine, combining catchy melodies with poignant lyrics, showing a balance between bravado and vulnerability. That is what works so well in the album: the immense vulnerability in the lyrics and vocals contrasted with the driving pace of the tracks. Two working-class lads who did plenty of drugs but understood the human soul and managed to get ordinary people to sing "Wonderwall" to their partners for decades (full disclosure, I love it).

One of the defining features of the album is its immediacy. Songs like "Live Forever" and "Supersonic" burst forth with infectious energy that cannot be ignored. The lyrics written by Noel Gallagher are a mixture of arrogant swagger and honest introspection, capturing the perceptions, dreams, and frustrations of working-class youth in 90s Britain.

Decades after its release, the album continues to resonate, with timeless themes of youth, rebellion, and longing that transcend boundaries of time and space. Whether you are a diehard fan or a curious newcomer, this album remains a must-listen for anyone who loves genuine, unapologetic rock 'n' roll. The album is a cultural landmark that continues to inspire and captivate audiences with its electrifying blend of melody, attitude, and passion. Oasis may have come and gone, but their music lives on, forever etched in the annals of rock history.
