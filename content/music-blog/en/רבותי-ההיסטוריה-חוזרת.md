Lately I feel that we are trapped in a repeating cycle where everything goes on the same way over and over again, and maybe, without noticing, we ourselves create it. It seems we are back to living in the 90s and early 2000s. Suddenly there is a nostalgic awakening towards series like "Lost" and "The Pajamas", and bands like "The Porcupines", who disappeared from the stages even before I knew how to walk, return to an exciting comeback. Middling popular songs like David Guetta's I'm Good are given new life in remastered versions. Even the old Israeli game "Fifoosh" received a restoration and a relaunch.

In a broader perspective, it seems that in other aspects we are also recreating the past: the modern Palmach operated again this year in Syria, we find ourselves entering another battle in Lebanon, and being attacked by ballistic missiles originating in Iraq and Iran, only this time at least there is no fear of chemicals and the terrifying IDF masks. And the most surprising - Oasis, who would have believed, is once again creating a global uproar with the sale of tickets for their shows, although I'm not convinced that more than 2% of the British people even tried to get a ticket.

There are moments when I look at all this and ask myself: what is actually happening here? Are we really stuck in the past, or is it a basic human tendency - to color everything in shades of nostalgia? Is this feeling so deep and rooted in us? I don't have a clear answer, but first of all, I want to tell you a short story.

When I was in elementary school, I participated in a special program for (m)nerds, where instead of sitting in class every Wednesday, I would go to a place where they learn differently. There were no tests, no pressures, just enrichment for the sake of enrichment - a revolutionary and dangerous concept that can, mercifully, make children learn out of genuine curiosity. Or as Shemer Mauser described the traditional study methods against <a href="https://dai.ly/x2qsl7l" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 underline hover:text-indigo-500 font-medium break-all">(Arthur season 16 episode 193)</a> - "Another rare childhood charm that becomes a method Pavlovian reinforcements".

As a classic geek, I loved the math puzzle classes, detective puzzles, physics of music, drawing, zoology, website building, robotics and more. Suddenly, I had a day of the week where I both enjoyed and learned something new. But as in any setting, there were also lessons that were a little less routine. One of them involved watching the movie Vertical Limit – not exactly a masterpiece – in small parts, while discussing its moral dilemmas. It took us almost six months to "finish" this bad movie, but between us, it was still better than a regular class at my elementary school.

But the lesson that has stayed with me to this day was a lesson called "Adash group" - Land through songs. We learned Israeli history through Palmach songs, underground songs and war songs. Bab al-Wad, for example, and the immortal hit "Gentlemen, history repeats" by Haim Hefer. This poem, with its irony, was my first encounter with the question of free choice and the circularity of history. As a child, I wondered if everything really repeats itself, and if so - what is the significance of our choices in all of this?

These thoughts continued to accompany me in the background, while my life continued as usual. I played on PlayStation, went out with friends, life flowed. But now that I'm older, it's hard not to feel the same feeling - that history really does repeat itself. So if history repeats itself, maybe we should also bring back the good things that were.

<blockquote dir="auto">
"Gentlemen, history repeats itself
Nothing is lost, nothing is forgotten.
We will remember, under lead rain
How did the Palmach march in Syria.'
</blockquote>

This year was the 25th anniversary of "Silent Engines" - an album that continues to resonate not only thanks to the music but also thanks to the feelings it manages to evoke, especially in a time when reality itself feels like an endless circle. Among all the hits that this album gave us, "Dark Dream" caught me in particular, precisely because it speaks of this feeling of living in a loop - a feeling that cannot be ignored today.

Assaf Amdorski said that the song began as a protest song about money and work, but over time it developed and became something deeper and more personal, with the connection to the tragedy of the late Inbal Perlmutter. The text of the song depicts a character who supposedly has lost all meaning, who only asks for an end or a new beginning. Through the words and the melody, the feeling of emptiness, pangs of conscience and hopelessness blends with the character of Inbal, whose recording became a heart The beat of the song collected a number -

###### "When I wrote the song, it started as a protest song about money and work. Then friends told me that this style was not so suitable for me. They said the melody was beautiful, but they advised me to change the words, so I started cutting the words and putting them together again, and when I saw how the song was built, I realized that it was a little about Inbal's story, her death and what she symbolized for me."

<blockquote dir="auto">
"Something inside
Already dead years ago
And the noise in the head
Not enough to breathe
Lots of guilt
A lot of pangs of conscience
And a pit of emptiness.'
...
"The headlines echo in the newspapers
And the heart broke
And everything remains
Pale as ever.'
</blockquote>

When I heard Inbal's voice, especially with Assaf's sample slowing down her speech, these feelings touched me strongly. Her simple and perhaps even childish words - which she said while the country was still trying to digest Rabin's murder - sound like a painful call in the face of the absurdity of life. "We're all so cynical all the time," she said, "opening a newspaper and saying well, another disaster, but it's not like that." And really, something in these words sounds like a painful reflection on the human ability to normalize anything, even things that should not be normalized.

Maybe this is where the dog is buried. It is possible that the circle we feel, the feeling that history is repeating itself, is due to the fact that we are constantly repressing and normalizing. Feelings become indifferent, and we lose the ability to dream about something different, about real change. But it's not like that

But maybe history doesn't repeat itself. Maybe we just live in a moment where there is no hope. I recently heard Etgar Kerat say on a podcast that we live in a time where everyone feels like they are losing. I'm not sure he's right. It seems to me that we live in a time when no one believes that anything can change. Once upon a time, even a rhinoceros like Aviv Gefen could take a song like Imagine and make a version of it that would demand peace, something that today has become an almost obscene concept. To produce some kind of protest song that really sweeps, to put up a mirror that says in the simplest terms what the future is? What do we want to happen, where is the hope?

<blockquote dir="auto">
We will conquer the peace
and not the territories
let's try
Until it's good
until it is
</blockquote>

But today? Instead of a demand for peace, we repeatedly get stuck in headlines, in fears, and most of all - in the inability to dream about something else.

One of the bands I discovered this year and really connected with is The Diaspora Yeshiva Band. One of the standout songs they perform is Dreams Come True, originally written by Abie Rotenberg. This song touches the heart, talks about a Jewish man who dreams and does everything he can to ensure freedom for his brothers who are under oppression - especially in the context of the Jews who lived behind the Iron Curtain. It expresses the sense of responsibility and the deep connection between Jews around the world, even those who have never met. This song, which ends with the feeling of a dream coming true, reminds me how important it is to hold on to hope - even when it seems that reality does not allow it.

<blockquote dir="auto">
How I dreamed that one day we could be together,
Doing all the things that brothers do.
But I feared that I would have to dream forever,
For only dreamers still believe that dreams come true.
</blockquote>

So I'll finish, again, hopefully. In the hope that all the abductees will return home, and that we can feel wholeheartedly that someone is really doing everything to make this happen - not just organizing ceremonies that no one asked for, and using songs without asking the artists. Hoping that this war will end soon, and that we will see better days.

Because as Inbal said, "but it's not like that" but maybe, maybe it could be different.
