Today instead of writing about a single album, I will write about a playlist I made that revolved around Sir James Paul McCartney following his announcement that he was going on another tour at the age of 81(!). The playlist consists only of the works from Hesser's solo career since I believe you already know most of his works about black birds from the Beatles.

The playlist starts with Nineteen Hundred and Eighty Five - in my youth there was a long period when I was on the axis in Beer Sheva Tel Aviv and even lived there for a while. I was most of the time in the student bubble of Beer Sheva, a bubble that has a lot of energy but lacks a little character and is very homogeneous. But still there are cool bright spots in Be'er Sheva that I'm glad I got to know. There is one bar called "Perla" in the old city, a place without many games and poses, only one type of beer, good music and full of broken people. The first time I sat there I really fell in love with the place, as a Tel Avivian who grew up and lived only in Tel Aviv I didn't believe that Be'er Sheva could have a place with character. I am always alert in bars for the music that is playing, when a song that I like plays I immediately with the elegance of a dead swan and discreetly activate the Shazam.

That evening, Nineteen Hundred and Eighty Five was playing in the background, I turned on Shazam in the usual way and it appeared to me that it was Paul McCartney, I was sure at first that it was a mistake, more than once and twice he had already been wrong. I went to the bar to take a homicide after exactly 11 minutes,

I approached the person in charge of the music and inquired in front of him, and indeed I found out that it was indeed Paul McCartney. I was shocked at that moment, because I know the Beatles on Buria and Paul McCartney. I was blown away, how much versatility there is in this man who can surprise every time.

Nineteen Hundred and Eighty Five is a song full of character, energetic, infectious and wild. It has a pulsing synthesizer riff that very quickly sets the tone. It captures the spirit of the rock music scene of the 70s, mixing elements of rock, pop and even touches of glam rock. That's why I chose to put this song first in the playlist.

After that we continue to the opening song of the album London Town - it is a song that if I probably told AI to Gennert a Paul McCartney song he would write something in his style. I also bought this album on my trip to Chicago, about which I already wrote and expanded in a post about <a href="https://www.ohadleshno.com/post/graham-nash-songs-for-beginners" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 underline hover:text-indigo-500 font-medium break-all">Graham Nash</a>. In the playlist I put a lot of songs from the album (Famous groupies, Children Children, Deliver you children, cafe on the left bank) and I think it's simply excellent and worth hearing on its own.

The fact that music has become something where a modern pop star and a rapper can collaborate with a Beatle makes me so happy. Those who know the Leshno family know that James Bond is an iron sheep property of the family, my older brother Ari has all the James Bond movies on DVD. As a child I remember my family being obsessed with finding and watching all the old Roger Moore movies, even the wackiest ones. One of the most important things in James Bond films beyond Q and his inventions, women and British elegance, is the film's soundtrack. live and let die was composed by none other than paul mccartney, whose theme song has become a work that is probably much better known than the film itself. But, do you even know the guns and roses version of the theme song.

I tried to insert more songs from all kinds of Paul's albums and styles, from the songs with his wife Linda who was a super significant character in the plot of his life to songs from his latest albums like new. Hope you find out that the guy has done a few more things since he wrote "Atmol" and "Lo Yahi".
