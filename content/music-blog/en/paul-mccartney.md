Today instead of writing about a single album, I created a playlist centered around Sir James Paul McCartney following his announcement of another tour at age 81(!). The playlist consists solely of works from Sir Paul's solo career, as I assume you are already familiar with his Beatles classics like "Blackbird".

The playlist begins with "Nineteen Hundred and Eighty-Five". For a long period I traveled the Beersheba-Tel Aviv route and lived there briefly. Most of the time I stayed in the student bubble of Beersheba: energetic, slightly lacking character, and homogeneous. Yet there were cool spots in Beersheba I was glad to discover. There was a bar called Perla in the Old City: no nonsense, one type of beer, great music, and plenty of characters. The first time I sat there I loved the place: as a Tel Aviver who grew up almost exclusively in Tel Aviv, I couldn't believe a spot with such character existed in Beersheba. I am always attentive to music playing in bars, and when a track I love plays, I subtly open Shazam.

That evening "Nineteen Hundred and Eighty-Five" played in the background. I opened Shazam and it identified Paul McCartney. Initially I thought it was a Shazam error because it had failed me before. I walked up to the bartender after eleven minutes to confirm, and he verified it was indeed Paul McCartney. I was stunned: I knew the Beatles and McCartney inside out. My mind was blown by the versatility of a musician who continuously surprises you.

"Nineteen Hundred and Eighty-Five" is full of character: energetic, infectious, and wild. It features a pulsing synth riff that sets the tone instantly, capturing 70s rock atmosphere while blending rock, pop, and glam rock touches. That is why I placed it first on the playlist.

Next we continue to the title track of "London Town", a song that feels like what AI would generate if asked to create a Paul McCartney track. I bought this record during my Chicago trip which I wrote about in my <a href="https://www.ohadleshno.com/post/graham-nash-songs-for-beginners" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 underline hover:text-indigo-500 font-medium break-all">Graham Nash post</a>. I included several tracks from that album in the playlist (Famous Groupies, Children Children, Deliver Your Children, Cafe on the Left Bank) because it is a stellar album worth listening to in full.

I added collaborations with popular artists like Kanye West and Stevie Wonder, who operate in vastly different genres. The fact that modern pop stars and rappers collaborate with a Beatle makes me happy. Music is a living entity, and seeing these blends is heartwarming. Anyone familiar with the Leshno family knows James Bond is a household staple: my older brother Ari owns every James Bond movie on DVD. As a kid I remember our family obsession with watching all the old movies, even the wilder Roger Moore ones. Beyond Q's gadgets, women, and British elegance, the soundtrack is vital. "Live and Let Die" was composed by Paul McCartney, and its title theme became more famous than the film itself (though you might know Guns N' Roses' cover).

I tried to include tracks across Paul's diverse albums and eras, from songs with his late wife Linda, who was a pivotal figure in his life, to tracks from recent albums like New. Hopefully you will discover that the man accomplished a few things since writing "Yesterday" and "Let It Be".

Enjoy the music.
