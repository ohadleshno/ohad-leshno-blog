I dedicate this post to my sister Yael, who set off on a new path in the United States with her family. I wish her "all the best", as our grandmother used to say, and that she will be happy in her new country. I remember when I drafted into the army, she made me a huge sign with the word "Embarrassments". So please, accept some embarrassment back.

<figure class="my-8"><img src="https://static.wixstatic.com/media/cd66a9_7e495dc3efeb46b9b9c554483e527beb~mv2.jpg" alt="" class="w-full h-auto rounded-2xl shadow-lg border border-slate-200 dark:border-slate-800 object-cover max-h-[550px]" /></figure>

Yoni Rechter is a living legend: his songs are such strong classics in Israeli culture that they define Israeliness at times. Today's album I am going to talk about is Yoni's first solo album, "Intentions" (Hitkavanut).

Yoni belongs to those artists who lack star mannerisms. He does not think about makeup or the right outfit or any traits that characterize singers and rock stars. He made the album first and foremost for himself, to present his core beliefs, knowing that the songs are complex and lack commercial potential, especially since three of the ten tracks are instrumental.

The album features the great classic "She's Here Again" (Shuv Hi Kan). I invited my sister for her last birthday to an evening of two shows by Yoni Rechter and Shlomi Shaban at Beit Guvrin. The drive to the show was a bit tough: my sister and I joked that it was a shame I didn't get a ride with Yoni Rechter since he lives near me. Shlomi talked a bit about Yoni's "problematic" personality, saying Yoni refused to play a song he didn't write with Shlomi, so they were forced to compromise <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YJfXf_wvfu0&ab_channel=YrArchive" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 underline hover:text-indigo-500 font-medium break-all">and play a track that is a blend of "Everyone Says" and "She's Here Again".</a> The concert was fantastic, and it was funny seeing older folks who came for Yoni Rechter leave right away when Shlomi Shaban took the stage.

On this album Yoni worked for the first time with Ali Mohar. Ali Mohar is perhaps one of my favorite lyricists in modern culture: he wrote most of Yoni's songs over the years. Ali said of himself:

<blockquote dir="auto">
"One of my attempts is to write simply. To write simply about complicated things at times. That is to say, to reach something true, which is sometimes hidden."
</blockquote>

In the nineties there was <a href="https://archive.kan.org.il/main/categories/music/8023/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 underline hover:text-indigo-500 font-medium break-all">a special program dedicated to the collaboration of these two creators</a> who produced endless beloved classics. On this album he wrote almost all the songs except "Rain Falling" written by Dan Minster ("Angels' Tears" etc.). For the song "That's All There Is", which was the first song they wrote together, there is <a href="https://archive.kan.org.il/main/special-projects/one-song-podcast/4791/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 underline hover:text-indigo-500 font-medium break-all">a podcast episode on One Song</a>.

The album encompasses several stances: it begins with a playful, clownish stance in "Don't Know Each Other" at the start of the album. The song describes a man trying awkwardly to hit on someone, with a pinch of chauvinism fitting the spirit of the era.

<blockquote dir="auto">
"Well she told me listen you're quite cute
I received the sentence
simply, yes simply
and I sat beside her
quite close, quite close
...
She nudged me with an elbow
and asked me what do you think
listen we don't know each other at all"
</blockquote>

Afterwards he leaves the playfulness and tries to explore genuine emotion. Like a process of peeling back human layers: at first "a beautiful day" and cynicism, and as we advance we enter deeper layers of personality until reaching the final song on the album, "Night and Day", with immense regret revealed.

<blockquote dir="auto">
"Night and day
what have I done
even had I wanted, I didn't fulfill
one small possibility,
among choices
I did not choose
I gave up on a small love
and only a cloud remained."
</blockquote>

The record sleeve shows Rechter sitting at a piano, his body almost hidden in a dark space. A single beam of light from an unknown source highlights the sheet music and keys, leaving the background in complete darkness. All this after he was already one of the greatest artists in the country, part of Kaveret, and involved in producing the greatest Israeli album of all time, "The Sixteenth Sheep". The statement of the album cover says everything about a person whose stature is admirable. But to be honest, all his accomplishments pale next to the fantastic cover of <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tJSNFw-SeMY&t=1651s&ab_channel=%D7%9B%D7%90%D7%9F11-%D7%AA%D7%90%D7%92%D7%99%D7%93%D7%94%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%93%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%94%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%9C%D7%99" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 underline hover:text-indigo-500 font-medium break-all">Million Dollars at the Panther Festival in 2020</a> with Eli Degibri.

Enjoy the music and good luck surviving the last week of August!
