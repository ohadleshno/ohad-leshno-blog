I dedicate this post to my sister Yael, who started a new journey in the United States with her family. I wish her "all the best", as our grandmother used to say, and that she be happy in her new country. I remember at my recruitment, when she made me a huge sign with the word "shame". So, please, get some shame back.

Yoni Rechter is a legend in his life, his songs are such strong classics in Israeli culture that they are the definition of Israeliness sometimes. The album today that I'm going to talk about is Yoni's first solo album, "Hatshevin".

יוני שייך לאותם אמנים שנעדרים אותם גינונים של כוכבים. He doesn't think about make-up or the right clothes or all the markers that characterize singers and rock artists. He made the album first and foremost for himself, to show his "I believe", and from the knowledge that the songs are complex and do not have commercial potential, in particular that three of the ten songs are instrumental pieces.

The album contains the big classic "she's here again".  I invited my sister, for her last birthday, to an evening of two performances by Yoni Rechter and Shlomi Shaban at Beit Govrin. The trip to the concert was a bit rough. My sister and I laughed about it. It's a shame I didn't already take a ride with Yoni Rechter because he lives near me. Shlomi, talked a bit about Yoni's "problematic" character, that he did not agree to play with Shlomi a song that he did not write, so they "had" to make a compromise and play a song that is a combination of "everyone says" and "She's here again".  The performance was excellent and it was funny to see all the old men who came to Yoni Rechter's performance and went straight to see Shlomi Shaban.

On this album Yoni worked for the first time with Ali Mohr. עלי מוהר הוא אולי אחד מהפזמונאים האהובים עלי בתרבות המודרנית, הוא כתב את רוב השירים של יוני במרוצת השנים. Ali testifies to himself:

<blockquote dir="auto">
"One of my experiments is to write simply. To write simply about complicated things sometimes. I mean, to get to something real, which is sometimes hidden."
</blockquote>

In the nineties there was a special program for the collaboration of the two creators who created countless beloved classics. In this album he wrote almost all the songs except for "Rain falling" written by Dan Minster ("Tears of Angels" etc.). On the song "That's all there is", which was the first song they wrote together, there is a chapter of one song.

The album contains several positions, it starts with some one position, which is perhaps a bit clownish in the song "You don't know each other" at the beginning of the album. The song talks about a man who tries to start with someone in a failed way, and of course there is a pinch of chauvinistic taste in it according to the spirit of the time.

<blockquote dir="auto">
"Well, she told me, listen, you're pretty cute
I got a sentence
In simplicity yes simplicity
And I sat next to her
pretty tight pretty tight
...
She elbowed me
And tell me what you think
Listen, we don't know each other at all."
</blockquote>

Then he leaves the clowning, and tries to wonder about real emotion. Like some process of peeling off the person, at first the "day is beautiful" and everything is good and a bit of cynicism. As we progress we enter deeper layers of the personality until we reach the last song on the album "Night and Day" with a huge regret that is revealed.

<blockquote dir="auto">
"Night and day
what did i do
Even if I wanted to, I didn't run out
One small option,
among the choices
I didn't choose
I gave up on a little love
And only a cloud remains.'
</blockquote>

The album cover shows Rechter sitting in front of a piano, his body almost hidden from view in a dark space. A single beam of light, coming from an unknown source, highlights the notes and keys of the piano, while leaving the background in complete darkness. All this after he was already one of the greatest artists in Israel. Was part of a beehive, took part in the production of the best Israeli album of all time "The Sixteenth Lamb". The saying of the cover says it all about the man who exalts him as an admirable person. But to be honest, all his work is dwarfed by the excellent cover for a million dollars at the Panther Festival in 2020 with Eli Degiberi.

Enjoy listening and good luck surviving the last week of August. (Or as my wonderful partner always says, "No Daghost")
