Following the holidays and the Sukkot holiday, the battle came to us and I remembered a Shabbat yeshiva I had somewhere in the late summer of 2013. The concept is still unclear to me to this day, basically we went to a boarding school to host all the senior levels of the high school yeshiva on Shabbat. On paper it sounds fun, all the friends stay together on Shabbat in some sloppy place with mediocre food, what could be bad? The problem is that the framework is full of a lot of uninteresting content that you must have. One of them is a third meal, for those who do not know Shabbat they are supposed to have three meals, in the evening after Arabic, the day after dawn and the last one before sunset. Many religious people have adopted the custom of having a relatively small meal on the third meal and singing religious songs.

I am with my influences and complexes, it was always very difficult for me to connect with all the religious ceremonies. Always in prayers I just waited for the time to pass, I didn't connect with the texts and I always felt terribly miserable and distant. On that Shabbat yeshiva, there was a show of questions and answers in front of the head of the yeshiva, from a platform to ask seemingly difficult questions.

They asked all the generic questions: "Where was God in the Holocaust?", "Is there free choice?" And we always had someone in the class who said that his grandmother saw a demon and asked: "Do demons exist?". I also went up to the court, and somewhat defiantly asked a question about the third meal in the style: "Why do we have to suffer with bad songs every Shabbat?". The question was followed by a heated discussion in which the conclusion of the discussion was that this is the ideal way and I'm just not yet connected enough to it to understand it. I'm not sure what I was trying to get from that question, because I already knew what the answer would be, but I think I wanted to tell myself that I don't fit in there, this world doesn't fit me.

A guy from the layer above me who I wasn't in touch with until that point approached me after the discussion and said: "Listen, it's not that they're wrong, but when I hear Idan Amadi I'm more connected to the Creator." So yes of course you can and should come down on him for the age of standing, but his point is clear. It took me several years to come to this deeper insight after all my life I thought I was not a "spiritual" person. Later I realized that my spiritual journey is manifested in other ways, one of which we will talk about today. Harrison's album All Things Must Pass is a spiritual journey that combines Eastern and Western culture as only a Beatle like George Harrison knows how to do. But before we dive into the spiritual part, we will first do a brief overview of the album and its background.

This is his first solo album written after the breakup of the Beatles in 1970, and it contains many references to their breakup.

It starts with the album cover which is photographed friar park in the yard of the mansion he bought. There he sits alone with four dwarves around him symbolizing the Beatles, he sits alone and tries to present liberation from them. And expressed in many songs such as Isn't it a pity (which the Beatles didn't want) that wonders how intelligent beings can cause each other so much suffering:

<blockquote dir="auto">
“Isn't it a pity?
Now isn't it a shame?
How we break each other's hearts and cause each other pain
How we take each other's love without thinking anymore
Forgetting to give back
Isn't it a pity?”
</blockquote>

And the song wah wah that was written during a break that took what beatles did during the get back session, expressing the murky atmosphere of the band in those days. You can really hear how sad George is about the band's situation, it's amazing how he manages to express his feelings in such an honest and beautiful way at that very moment.

Another example is in Run of the Mile, before the start of the get back sessions in 1968, George went to visit the greatest of them all and who influenced him a lot, Bob Dylan. There he met the band, George was so enthusiastic about their musical style and the song is an attempt to write in their style. In the song he blames apple crops for the bad influence they have on the band.

After we broadly understood the atmosphere of the album, with the biggest crisis in his life with the dissolution of the Beatles, in addition to the death of his mother and his journey to India and the connection to Hari Krishna. Now we can now dive into the more spiritual songs on the album. George deals a lot in the album with our relationship with God, he starts with the most famous song on the album is probably on the album my sweet lord, which was initially released as a single and broke the charts. George's desire in the song is that we connect to God each in his own way and it doesn't matter the religious platform he uses. He combines a style of gospel songs with Mahari Krishna influences, he uses words like "Hallelujah" and on the other hand recites the Maha-Mantra (the Great Mantra). A sixteen-word meditative prayer consisting of the Sanskrit names of God. Later George admitted that he was based on the gospel song Oh Happy Day and later lost a plagiarism lawsuit over the song.

And he continues with hear me lord which the Beatles didn't want either and he even has a version of it in the get back session.  He asks God for forgiveness for all the years that he ignored him and only wants him to hear him and help him ascend. A motif found in many religious songs in all religions. And he did not refrain from writing a love song in what is life with the opening riff so attractive in which he writes a love song to God.

<blockquote dir="auto">
"Beware of sadness
It can hit you, it can hurt you
Make you sore and what is more
That is not what we are here for"
</blockquote>

And even in such a serious album that contains George's absolute explosion, we find himself exposed to a bit of self-humor in the song I dig love. Where Harrison just sings about how he loves the male penis. How he loves him in the morning, how he loves him every night and he just wants us to know it.

In the original release, the album was divided into three albums, the third album is a collection of jam pieces that probably come as a counter to the Beatles and their super schematic method that shaped pop forever. He came to break and shake us to be informal and tell us Thanks for the Pepperoni.

Finally, let's mention the song that for me is the highlight of the album, all things must pass, another attempt by George to write a song in the style of the band, Harrison writes in a way that would not put writers like Dylan to shame. He reminds us of the very simple truth that we always try to escape from, in the end everything passes. The bad things, the good things all pass in the end and we too are among them.

"All things must pass, so I must be on my way 
The darkness only stay the night time in the morning it will pass away"
