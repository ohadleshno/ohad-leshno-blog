When I want to listen to music I act like a greedy algorithm. The most accessible thing that crosses my threshold is what I will hear. If I'm at home and there's a record already on the turntable, I'll play it. If I have an album on Apple Music that was played and stopped in the middle, I will continue to listen to it. I usually don't even think before I put music on I just continue what was there before. There are times when I find myself listening to the same albums over and over, dozens of times, until I move on to the next thing. The beautiful side of this is that each album is linked in my head to a specific period. For example, this album is linked to me in February 2020, to my trip to Budapest, exactly one month before the outbreak of the Corona epidemic. But before that, as usual, we will do a brief overview of the artist.

Geva Alon was born and raised in Kibbutz Mabarot. He started playing the guitar at the age of 12. In 1999, he started his career as a vocalist in the band "The Flying Baby" (which will appear in Barbie soon). One of the things that ended up breaking into Giva Alon's consciousness was David Bowie's cover of Modern Love. I feel that many times sinful covers do not bring anything new, and if they do, it would be better if they didn't, I can't understand the love that surrounds all the covers of all the reality shows. Here Geva Alon brings the song in a really new variation as if it was originally written for him. Except that I love the fact that he published it like some boomer with a picture of roses and didn't even remove the wmv suffix from the name. He didn't do the minimum of the minimum and that's beautiful. I feel like I accidentally entered a video that explains how to adjust the computer's network settings from some virgin boy who runs the Maple forum on FXP.

Back to my personal story, although I love adventure, travel and world experiences, I always find myself debating whether I'm really enjoying myself. I have a misconception that when I'm abroad I have to have fun, and if I'm not having fun, I try hard to have fun, which makes me even more desperate. When I was a guide at Bnei Akiva, it was easy to spot the kids who wanted attention. They did everything to get some attention, but the more annoying they behaved, the less attention they got from the guides. Their behavior was annoying and repulsive, and the harder they tried, the less they got what they wanted. The attention they wanted, they had to understand that this is not the right way. I realized that I must be less accomplished, and there is no right or wrong answer. But, when you face this thought in an honest way, I thought that if I don't like to travel, I am a boring and meaningless person who is destined to live a boring life. The social perception took a huge toll on me. As I was brought up, if I didn't succeed, I went to another museum, another bar, but every time I didn't achieve my goal, I had to learn to let go. That night, we got organized in our Airbnb and I just sat down and put it This album was on TV, and it was a sweet comfort and I said to myself that I wanted to stay in the apartment. It was the best evening of the trip. It's funny in retrospect how the world signaled to us all that we had to stop in such a symbolic way.

I think it's not for nothing that this album gave me the ability to stop for a second and breathe. It opens with "The great Enlightenment", and sets a thoughtful tone that permeates all the other songs on the album. Alon's guitar work is extraordinary, it weaves complex patterns that are both soothing and stimulating. His voice, with its warm fluctuation, carries a sense of seriousness and intimacy, and invites the listener on a personal journey. It allows the listener to really connect with himself for a second and be with himself for a second in the moment. It reaches a high point in the album for me in the song which is the theme song of the album "Grey herons". Meticulously and beautifully executed, poetic words of hope and renewal. The sparse processing allows Alon's voice and guitar to fill the space with emotional resonance.

All the other songs on the album are great. In particular "I See the Love" shows Alon's ability to seamlessly combine folk and rock elements. His use of reverb on the guitar creates a kind of atmospheric sound, while the subtle use of slide guitar adds a touch of such fun Americana.

The album was signed with the song "Rock All Night" which is a tribute to classic rock with a touch of folk. This song features a powerful rock sound with a full band, which adds a rough texture to the album. It's a song calling to celebrate life, packed with vibrant energy and raw electric guitar solos.

Before my flight to Vietnam, I went to his performance at Nocturno in Jerusalem, one of the best performances I've seen recently, he is a virtuoso in a way unmatched in Israel in my opinion, a one man show that feels like a whole band. During the show he made a speech in which he said that this was his first performance since the start of the war. See his pain, his fear, his humanity, he was exposed and honest and brought it all to the stage. And I, too, there in the experience, I suddenly encountered a familiar feeling, like back then on vacation, my feeling wonders what I'm doing. I started to ask myself, am I even enjoying myself? Is it allowed to enjoy the current situation in the country? But this time I came to her prepared, I reassured myself with the inner saying that it's okay if I go to the show and I don't enjoy it. And if it's not good then it's all good, I can let go. At that moment something magical happened, I gave myself the ability to really let go, concentrate on the music and forget this thought as if it didn't exist. It was a full circle for me once again with Alon.
