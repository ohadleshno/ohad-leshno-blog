This week we celebrated Valentine's Day on February 14th, and I wondered: why do we call it Valentine's Day? Why does love get a dedicated day? I wondered about the origin of the holiday. After deep research involving Wikipedia, a Blazer article on top 21st-century women, and two YouTube videos on dating advice, here is the summary. Like everything in Western culture, Valentine's Day began as a Christian commemoration for Saint Valentine, eventually becoming a popular holiday marking romantic love.

<blockquote dir="auto">
"It is said one of these saints performed secret marriages for Christian Roman soldiers forbidden to marry under Emperor Claudius II, and was executed as a result. Before his death he wrote a letter to the jailer's daughter whom he cured of blindness, signing it 'Your Valentine'."
</blockquote>

While couples worldwide celebrate love on this holiday, it is worth remembering not every love story ends happily. For some, Valentine's Day is a painful reminder of lost love, dissolved relationships, and broken hearts. Because I am cynical and struggle to digest commercialized romance without criticism, I thought about what album would fit best without causing nausea. Enter Tom Waits, the raspy-voiced troubadour whose music speaks to the heartbroken. His debut album, "Closing Time", is a masterpiece of melancholy and yearning, perfect for navigating heartbreak. (Gil Matos hosted a great radio special on this album worth checking out).

In an ever-shifting musical landscape, certain albums stand as monuments to an era, capturing its spirit beyond time. Tom Waits' debut, "Closing Time", released in 1973, is one such masterpiece. Serving as an introduction to Waits' unique blend of jazz, blues, and folk, it laid the foundation for his legendary career. What makes "Closing Time" truly timeless is its universality. While rooted in the 1970s Los Angeles music scene, its themes of love and loss remain as relevant today as five decades ago. In an era of constant change, there is comfort in revisiting Waits' weary vocals, knowing that even as the world changes, certain truths remain constant.

At its core, "Closing Time" is a journey through the dim, smoky alleys of the human experience. From the mournful longing of "Ol' 55" to the elegiac balladry of "Martha", Waits weaves an emotional tapestry resonating deeply with listeners. Every song feels like a frozen snapshot capturing the beauty and pain of life. On "Grapefruit Moon", Waits paints a vivid portrait of lost love and redemption. The melancholic melody and evocative lyrics depict heartbreak and the yearning for a second chance. Yet amidst darkness, a flicker of hope shines as the narrator finds solace in nature: a grapefruit moon hanging in the sky. Conversely, "Lonely" explores depths of isolation. With its plaintive melody and piercing lyrics, the song captures the pain of feeling disconnected, while offering empathy to those struggling. It serves as a reminder that loneliness is a universal human condition, and even in dark moments we are not alone. Meanwhile "Ice Cream Man" offers a playful departure from introspective tracks, presenting a whimsical vendor wandering nighttime streets to offer sweet treats to those needing comfort, reminding us to find moments of joy amidst hardship.

"Closing Time" is not merely about heartbreak: it is also about resilience. Despite the pain of lost love, the album reflects the human capacity to endure. Through tracks like "Martha" and "I Hope That I Don't Fall in Love With You", Waits illustrates resilience by diving into love's complexities. On "Martha", the protagonist reaches out to a past love, taking a trip down memory lane (<a href="https://www.ohadleshno.com/post/selective-hearing-telephone-operator-playlist" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 underline hover:text-indigo-500 font-medium break-all">which I covered previously</a>). It is a poignant reminder that even when love is lost, memories endure, shaping who we are. Similarly, "I Hope That I Don't Fall in Love With You" captures the vulnerability of opening one's heart despite fear of getting hurt. By acknowledging these feelings, Waits encourages listeners to embrace vulnerability and find strength in the capacity to love again.

<blockquote dir="auto">
"Well, I hope that I don't fall in love with you
'Cause falling in love just makes me blue
Well, the music plays and you display your heart for me to see
I had a beer and now I hear you calling out for me
And I hope that I don't fall in love with you"
</blockquote>

The album cover shows Tom alone at a bar table, his silhouette illuminated by soft light. His posture is relaxed yet reflective, conveying introspection and melancholy, harmonizing with the instrumental title track "Closing Time" that closes the album. Through somber piano and Waits' weary delivery, the track captures the bittersweet essence of bidding farewell to a love that wasn't meant to be. It reminds us that sometimes doors must close so others can open.

Wishing you all less cynicism and more love.
