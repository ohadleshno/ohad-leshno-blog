I remember the first time I heard a song by Izhar Ashdot. I was in 7th grade and one of his songs played on the radio. My older brother Ari was surprised that I didn't know the song, confident that I would fall in love with it. He promised to bring me the CD for the album "A Little Dance" (Rikud Katan). Thank God he brought me nothing, and somehow I managed to download it myself. Keep in mind that back then Spotify and other great services did not exist, so I visited very sketchy websites to download music. To this day I carry viruses from those sites.

I have a strange connection to this album. When I was in 8th grade I had an app on my phone that accidentally played the album at 1.75x speed. For some reason that was how I listened to the album for the first time, and I got used to it. To this day when I hear it at normal speed it sounds strange, so I prefer the sped-up version. At the time I listened to this album every morning on the way to high school. The album at that speed was exactly the length of my shared taxi ride on route 5 to my high school on Rothschild.

Izhar is the partner of actress Alona Kimhi, who starred in the legendary movies "Abba Ganuv" and "Abba Ganuv 2". Without them it would be hard for me to find temporary variable names. She wrote the lyrics for songs on the album "English Autumn": "The Ballad Of" and "Shadow of a Summer Day". All of Alona's songs are beautiful descriptions of fleeting moments, possessing a wonderful ability to penetrate the heart. For example, in "Shadow of a Summer Day":

<blockquote dir="auto">
"Searching for you everywhere
perhaps suddenly
you will be born out of a random moment
"
</blockquote>

<blockquote dir="auto">
"An old yearning
like an out of tune note
gives me no rest"
</blockquote>

I have a funny anecdote about the song "The Ballad Of", where at the time I was amused by the line:

<blockquote dir="auto">
"Oh orchid in a swamp, just another wild plant"
</blockquote>

As if Izhar had a strong desire to insult the swamp orchid as just another wild plant unrelated to the song. For years I walked around believing Izhar Ashdot hated swamp orchids. Years later I discovered the actual lyrics were "Oh orchid in a swamp, oh wild plant" (I submitted this once to the misheard lyrics site Avatiach, but it wasn't accepted).

The album "A Little Dance" exposed me for the first time to Irish style and culture. "A Little Dance" is also the title track. Izhar composed Yahli Sobol's lyrics for the Irish Festival in Tel Aviv. The song exposed me to Irish energy, fast rhythm, and storytelling that I find beautiful. Izhar also performs the legendary Irish folk song "Whiskey In The Jar" on the album, which even Metallica covered (a very bad cover, but <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y2nkQQkjCU8&ab_channel=JerryGarcia-Topic" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 underline hover:text-indigo-500 font-medium break-all">Jerry Garcia</a> has a wonderful cover).

Finally, the album closes with "At the End of Voice and Image", written by Yankale Rotblit and composed by Izhar. This song is a timeless classic, originally performed by Danny Bassan with Tislam. The music video from the show Zehu Ze is a phenomenon on its own: one woman's <a href="https://youtube.com/clip/Ugkx_T_ZGwsIRQc-Yxf9Z8EK99GDucX5jWOp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 underline hover:text-indigo-500 font-medium break-all">hair catches fire from a sparkler</a> and everyone continues as if nothing happened. Additionally, <a href="https://youtube.com/clip/UgkxTcLtZagYJb40osJT-u89Hd9ya5SneFFL" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 underline hover:text-indigo-500 font-medium break-all">Moni Moshonov</a> loses it in a guest appearance in the crowd. One of the wilder videos: recommended viewing.

To be honest, it is not an album destined for the pantheon, but it stays with me and I personally love Izhar Ashdot as a producer and artist. It marks for me that period of riding shared taxis to high school, one of the first albums I played endlessly, and to this day I love it dearly.

Enjoy the music.
