I remember the first time I heard a song by Yizhar Ashdot. I was in seventh grade and one of his songs was playing on the radio. My older brother, Ari, was surprised how I didn't know the song, and he was sure I would love it. He promised to bring me the CD of the album 'Little Dance'. And really, thank God, he didn't bring me anything, somehow I managed to get him down by myself. Let you know that in those days there was no spotify and the other good vegetables that are available today, and that's how I ended up on very dubious websites to download the songs. To this day I carry viruses on me from these sites.

So I have a rather strange thing about this album. When I was in the 8th grade I had an app on my phone, which accidentally played the album once at 1.75 speed. For some reason, that's how I heard this album for the first time and since then I've gotten used to it. To this day when I listen to it at normal speed it sounds strange to me, so I prefer the fast version. At that time I would listen to this album every morning on the way to high school. The album at this speed was exactly the length of time it took me to get on a service taxi of line five, and get all the way to Rothschild High School.

The album 'Little Dance' exposed me to Irish style and culture for the first time. 'Little Dance' is also the theme song of the album. Yizhar composed the words of Yali Sobol for the Irish Festival in Tel Aviv. This song exposed me to the Irish energies, the high tempo and this storytelling which is so beautiful in my eyes. Yizhar also performs the legendary Irish folk song Whiskey In The Jar on the album, which even Metallica covered (the cover is very bad, but hover:text-indigo-500 font-medium break-all">Gary Garcia</a> has a wonderful cover).

Finally, the album closes with the song 'Kachlot kol va halt', written by Yankela Rotblit and composed by Yizhar. This song is truly a timeless classic, and it was originally performed by Danny Bassan as part of the group 'Tislam'. It marks for me that time of the service taxi rides to high school, and is one of the first albums I listened to, and to this day I love it very much.
