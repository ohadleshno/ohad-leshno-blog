For a long time I wanted to write about a breakup, I feel that in the last two years I have experienced a lot of breakups, some of my initiative and some forced on me. Separation from a relationship, from people who were closest to me, a workplace that I loved terribly, people who moved away because of the force of life or just people who moved. Recently, unfortunately, even as someone who lives in a country like ours, I experienced a lot of loss, I saw a shocking loss in the people closest to me. A breakup is probably the hardest experience a person can go through. It involves immense pain, a severe sense of loss, and the recognition of the inability to communicate with the other party. This is probably the topic that has been the most discussed in music, and perhaps it is because there has not been a person in human history who has not experienced it in the strongest way, and it is not for nothing that people always tend to say "the most beautiful songs are the songs of Memorial Day".

Among all the songs, I noticed that it's interesting to think that our natural tendency after a breakup is to try to remember.  Many of the songs focus on memory, the power of our memory, and how much we preoccupy ourselves with thoughts about the past. I have a friend who always, always takes pictures of every social event we are at, sometimes it seems that the documentation has become an inseparable part of it, overriding any other desire. Once I asked him why he documents, his answer was simple: "I document because I want to remember, otherwise I will forget." And immediately I thought about this conflict between remembering and being, and I remembered the class I was in more than a decade ago by Elishev (who has a Telegram channel that is a ray of light in my life) about the mishna in the masks of Avota Tu.

<blockquote dir="auto">
Four measures sit before the sages: a sponge, and a funnel, a shifter, and a strainer.
</blockquote>

1. Absorbent, which absorbs everything.
1. A funnel, which puts in one and takes out another.
1. A shifter, which takes out the wine and collects the yeast.
1. And a sieve, which removes the flour and collects the semolina.

Elishav asked us which of the four types of students presented in the mishna is the best in our opinion. Of course everyone agreed that the ideal is to be like the sponge, because everything he learns he absorbs, that is, remembers. But then he challenged this perception, and presented us with a question - would we not take our baby to an amusement park even though he would not remember the experience? It was as clear to me at that moment as the sun will rise tomorrow, that the answer is no. My answer was so quick and thoughtless, like someone who is completely gripped by a mental conception and cannot get out of the mental prison. I thought that the value of memory surpasses any other value and that is the most important thing. But, at the same moment that the answer came out of my mouth, I understood what I said, and like a person who saw all the ancestors of impurity before him, something broke in me. Some kind of magical moment when the token fell to me, I realized, perhaps for the first time in my life, how much I really don't know, despite the illusion of my knowledge. In the same breath, I realized that living and moving forward are no less important values ​​than the memory of the past.

But still, sometimes the feeling after a breakup is that all we have left is the memory, and if we lose the memory the feeling is that we will finally lose everything. As in Gim's song Photographs and Memories (I wrote about it extensively on the blog) describes that all he has left is only the memory. And precisely because of this, there is a very powerful section in South Park where Butters sits crying on the sidewalk, the punks come to him and ask him what happened? He tells them his girlfriend dumped him. In an act of support they suggest that he join them in a cemetery and cry about how bitter the world is and how meaningless it is. He refuses, he doesn't think life is terrible, life is good according to him the fact that he now feels terrible means that he has lost something that has done him a lot of good and this knowledge that he can feel so good gives him hope.

Farewell gives us meaning to the thing we lost, from the place of nothingness it is impossible to appreciate what was. It lets us free ourselves from the things that bound us and move on to the next things, to go out and conquer the world. It's great that we can remember the good and embrace what was, but someday we'll have to face the harsh reality and move on. I learned from all the recent breakups that the most important thing is to give space to all the things that come up. Being in despair, in pain, in loneliness in loss, we can remember the good that was, understand that we cannot control what happened, but at the same time take renewed energy from this place and we will be able to move forward because if we managed to have this much pain then we can have so much better in the future. And if we act properly, maybe in a great Shemiah Seita we will be able to be like Sinatra and be able to say wholeheartedly Loves been good to me.
