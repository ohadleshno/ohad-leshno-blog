For a long time I wanted to write about breakups and farewells. I feel that in the past two years I experienced many partings: some initiated by me and some forced upon me. Parting from relationships, from people who were closest to me, from a workplace I loved dearly, people who grew distant due to life's flow, or simply people who relocated to another country. Recently, living in a country like ours, I unfortunately also experienced much loss and witnessed devastating loss among those closest to me. Parting is probably the hardest experience a person can undergo: it involves immense pain, a heavy sense of loss, and the realization of being unable to communicate with the other side. It is perhaps the most explored topic in music, likely because every human being in history has experienced it profoundly. It is no coincidence that people often say the most beautiful songs are Memorial Day songs.

Partings have many forms of expression in music:

- Songs about the breakup moment itself, focusing on what happened when the phone rang and your voice trembled.
- Anger and hatred arising from parting, like Russian Theater.
- The immense pain accompanying it, like One Soft Touch.
- Despair bordering on depression: I Used to Be a King, Dark Dark, Yesterday.
- Obsessive analysis of what happened and whether anything could fix it: Don't Think Twice It's All Right, If Only You Spoke, If I Ever Thought You've Changed Your Mind.
- Immense longing: Looks Like Rain, Martha.
- Calls to return to what was: Love of My Life, Don't Leave Me, Don't Go Now.
- Frustration: Why Does Love Got to Be So Sad?, Bye Bye Love, Goodbye to Love.
- Solitude: How Could I Ever Be Single Again, Rain Falling.
- Loss: After You Drove Away, Tears in Heaven.
- Concern for the other side: I Wonder If She's Fine.
- Melancholy: Have You Ever Seen the Rain?, Fill Another Cup My Friend.
- Telling yourself to stop crying: Stop Loving Her Today, Stop Crying Your Heart Out.

<blockquote dir="auto">
"I wonder if she's fine
Down in the corner of my mind"
</blockquote>

Among all these songs, I noticed our natural tendency after a breakup is trying to remember. Many songs focus on memory, the power of memory, and how much we dwell on thoughts of the past. I have a friend who films every social event we attend. At times documentation seems an inseparable part of him, overriding any other desire. Once I asked him why he documents everything, and his answer was simple: "I document because I want to remember, otherwise I will forget." I immediately thought about this conflict between remembering and being, recalling a class I attended over a decade ago taught by Elishav (<a href="https://t.me/elishav_rabinovich" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 underline hover:text-indigo-500 font-medium break-all">who runs a Telegram channel that is a ray of light in my life</a>) on Ethics of the Fathers (Pirkei Avot 5:15).

<blockquote dir="auto">
There are four types among those who sit before the wise: a sponge, a funnel, a strainer, and a sieve.
</blockquote>

1. A sponge, which absorbs everything.
2. A funnel, which lets in at one end and lets out at the other.
3. A strainer, which lets out the wine and retains the dregs.
4. A sieve, which lets out the coarse flour and retains the fine flour.

Elishav asked us which of the four types of students presented in the Mishnah is the best in our opinion. Unanimously everyone agreed the ideal is the sponge, because it absorbs everything it learns, meaning it remembers. But then he challenged that perception, asking us: wouldn't we take our child to an amusement park even if they won't remember the experience? It was clear to me at that moment like sunrise tomorrow that the answer is no. My initial answer was so hasty, like someone trapped in a rigid mindset unable to escape a mental prison. I thought the value of memory exceeded all other values. But the moment the answer left my mouth I realized what I said, and something broke inside me. A magical moment occurred where the penny dropped: I understood, perhaps for the first time in my life, how little I truly know despite my illusion of knowledge. In the same breath I realized that living and moving forward are values no less important than remembering the past.

Yet at times the feeling after a breakup is that all we hold in our hands is memory, and if we lose memory we lose everything. As Jim Croce described in "Photographs and Memories" (<a href="https://www.ohadleshno.com/post/jim-corce-you-don-t-mess-around-with-jim" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 underline hover:text-indigo-500 font-medium break-all">which I wrote about extensively on the blog</a>), all he has left is memory. Precisely because of this, there is a powerful scene in South Park where Butters sits crying on the sidewalk. Goth kids approach him asking what happened, and he tells them his girlfriend dumped him. In support they offer him to join them at the cemetery to weep over how dark and meaningless the world is. He refuses: he doesn't think life is terrible, asserting life is good, and feeling terrible now means he lost something that made him very happy, giving him hope.

A breakup gives meaning to what we lost: from absence we learn to appreciate what was. It allows us to release things that bound us and move forward to conquer the world. It is wonderful that we can remember the good and cherish the past, but eventually we must face harsh reality and continue onward. I learned from recent partings that the most important thing is giving room to whatever surfaces: despair, pain, loneliness, loss. We can recall the good that was, accept that we cannot control what happened, and simultaneously draw renewed energy to move forward. Because if we were capable of feeling so much pain, we are capable of feeling even greater joy in the future. And if we act properly, perhaps with great grace we will be like Sinatra and say with a full heart: Love's been good to me.

Enjoy the music, and may you merit to love anew.

Always remember:

<blockquote dir="auto">
"Look: just as time is not inside clocks, so love is not inside bodies, bodies merely manifest love."
</blockquote>
