Today we will talk about a fascinating album most of you likely don't know. Anyone who knows me knows how much I love folk artists, especially those with wild stories. Jim is a textbook folk artist: he started his career playing dive bars and small clubs across America, married in 1966, and converted to Judaism. He then sold his possessions and moved from New York to a farm in Pennsylvania. Playing every night for twenty-five dollars wasn't enough to pay bills, so he worked blue-collar jobs as a truck driver and guitar teacher.

Jim was an intriguing figure with a crazy life story. His non-standard perspective allowed him to observe details regular people miss. Jim had a tragic end, dying in a plane crash at age thirty. This breakthrough album is packed with treasures.

"You Don't Mess Around with Jim", the opening track and title song, launched Jim into the music world. The song tells the story of an incident in a New York pool hall: Jim is a hustler who cons a guy named Slim. Slim doesn't appreciate the con and stabs him to get his money back. Everyone knows there are things you don't do:

<blockquote dir="auto">
"You don't tug on <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superman" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 underline hover:text-indigo-500 font-medium break-all">Superman</a>'s cape / You don't spit into the wind / You don't pull the mask off that ol' <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lone_Ranger" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 underline hover:text-indigo-500 font-medium break-all">Lone Ranger</a> / And you don't mess around with Jim."
</blockquote>

The guitar in Jim's songs carries strong character, acting as an extra voice. The songs are magnificent and touching, especially when melodies blend harmoniously with storytelling. Here are great examples:

"Photographs and Memories", a song about memories remaining from a departed love:

<blockquote dir="auto">
"Photographs and memories
Christmas cards you sent to me
All that I have are these
To remember you"
</blockquote>

"Time in a Bottle", a beautiful love song:

<blockquote dir="auto">
"If I could save time in a bottle
The first thing that I'd like to do
Is to save every day till eternity passes away
Just to spend them with you"
</blockquote>

"A Long Time Ago", a song about an evening of great, innocent, pure love, an encounter marked by hesitation and restraint:

<blockquote dir="auto">
"I was so afraid to touch you
Thought you were too young too know
So I just watched you sleeping
Then you woke and said to me
The night is cold
It frightens me
And I could sleep so easy next to you"
</blockquote>

And in "Operator", Jim captures an atmosphere that no longer exists in the modern era. The song tells of a guy calling an operator to trace the number of his ex-girlfriend who moved to LA with his friend. He wants to call to say he moved on, but when he receives the number he cannot read it through tears, confessing to the operator that he cannot make the call. Jim drew inspiration for the lyrics during military service watching men line up at payphones to call home.

<blockquote dir="auto">
"So I can call just to tell 'em I'm fine
And to show I've overcome the blow
I've learned to take it well
I only wish my words could just convince myself
That it just wasn't real
But that's not the way it feels"
</blockquote>

I feel the album contains scenes with complex, immersive stories that throw you into the deep end. I highly recommend listening to more tracks from his discography.

Enjoy the music.
